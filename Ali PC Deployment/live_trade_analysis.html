The improvements were already applied — and this is what they are worth

Findings 2 and 3 are exactly what the configuration change on 11 Sep addressed: the fixed 1,111-tick stop was replaced with 1.25 × ATR(14), and supplementary adds were turned off (MAX_SUPP=0).

Over this identical window the old rules simulate to -$168.64; the current live rules to +$1,672.20 at PF 2.04. The account's real result under the old rules was +$66.10 — carried entirely by five lucky trades.

The one new idea I tested — and rejected

On these 11 live days, raising the histogram threshold from 15 to 20 looked excellent: +$1,881.58 versus +$1,672.20, profit factor 2.78 versus 2.04, and drawdown cut from $583 to $328.

It does not survive validation. Over 6 months, walk-forward:

TH 15 (current live)+$26,321.291.88-$1,493+$11,745.83

TH 20 gives up $3,544 out-of-sample. Eleven days is far too short to separate an edge from luck, and this is what over-fitting looks like when you catch it. No change recommended. TH 20 does buy a genuinely lower drawdown, so it is a defensible choice if you want a calmer ride — but it is a risk preference, not an improvement.

Caveats

Nothing here validates the current rules live. All 200 trades predate them; the live sample under the new configuration is still zero. The comparison above is simulation versus simulation.

Simulation is kinder than reality. The backtest fills at the break level; the live account paid real spread and slippage on every one of those 3-second stop-outs.
